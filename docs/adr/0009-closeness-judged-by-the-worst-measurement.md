# Closeness is judged by the worst measurement

Each measurement of a match gets a closeness percentage against its target: 100% at the middle, 80% at the edges of the target's range, then 10% less for every ⅛″ (3 mm) flat beyond the edge, down to 0%. A match's closeness is its worst measurement's, and ties go to the higher average. An average would let one bad measurement hide behind several good ones: ¼″ off everywhere would rank below perfect everywhere but ¾″ off at the leg opening, even though the first fits better.

## Weighted by importance

Users set an **importance** from 1 to 5 on each measurement, starting at 3. Each measurement's shortfall (100% minus its closeness) counts in proportion to its importance divided by the highest importance currently set. A match's closeness is 100% minus its largest weighted shortfall, and ties go to the smaller weighted average. With every importance equal, this ranks exactly as the plain worst measurement does, so the reason above still holds. A measurement at importance 1 still counts a little, which replaced an earlier "doesn't matter" switch that dropped it altogether. Importance never touches the minimum-ease hard limit or the inseam "at least" rule, and the closeness shown for each measurement stays unweighted.

## Consequences

- The ease table's ranges come mostly from estimates. Tests check them against the catalogue by comparing every cut with Levi's published body chart for its size. If the cuts brands call slim mostly land in regular, the snug and regular ranges move up by PR before the first release.
